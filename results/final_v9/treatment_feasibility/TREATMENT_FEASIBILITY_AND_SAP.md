# Treatment workstream: feasibility audit and proposed statistical analysis plan (draft for decision)

**Status.** Design only. No treatment-effect model has been run, and the manuscript is unchanged. The locked v8 analysis (stratified Cox M2a, validation C-index 0.6216, groups Favourable / Intermediate / Adverse with cut-points 0.3793 and 0.4756) is not altered. The risk groups were recomputed in the feasibility script and reproduced exactly; they are used here only as a descriptive stratifier. Tables H01–H10 in this folder are aggregate and were generated by `notebooks/treatment_feasibility_v9.py` and `notebooks/treatment_coverage_v9.py`.

The question is whether the registry supports a credible comparison of treatment strategies, and if so, which one. The answer below is: **a comparison of pre-operative chemotherapy for liver metastases against no pre-operative chemotherapy is the most defensible primary question. Surgery alone versus surgery plus chemotherapy, as usually stated, cannot be identified from these fields.** Several variables must be resolved before any estimate is produced.

---

## 2. Timing: before surgery, after surgery or both

Pre- and post-operative liver-chemotherapy flags (H02, H03):

| Pattern (cohort 17,262) | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-operative only | 3,826 | 22.2 |
| Post-operative only | 4,096 | 23.7 |
| Both pre- and post-operative | 3,659 | 21.2 |
| No recorded liver chemotherapy (No / No) | 3,417 | 19.8 |
| No recorded chemotherapy, with ND in one field | 1,533 | 8.9 |
| Unknown (ND or blank in both fields) | 731 | 4.2 |

Start dates agree with the flags. Of dated pre-operative chemotherapy, 4,259 of 4,320 (98.6%) started before the operation. Of dated post-operative chemotherapy, 4,337 of 4,425 (98.0%) started after the operation. Eighty-eight post-operative starts are dated before the operation, which is a data-quality issue.

**The primary-tumour chemotherapy field is not a "post-hepatectomy" variable.** Of 3,416 dated primary-tumour starts, 2,954 (86%) occurred before the liver operation, because metachronous and synchronous sequencing often places primary surgery first. This field therefore cannot be used to define "chemotherapy after surgery". It can be a covariate or a secondary exposure, but the meaning of "post-operative" differs between the two fields.

## 3. Missing dates, definitions, eligibility and immortal-time bias

**Missing dates.** About 42% of liver chemotherapy flags have no start date (pre-operative 3,165 of 7,485; post-operative 3,330 of 7,755). Pre-operative exposure does not need a date if it is defined as received before the operation, which the dated subset supports. Post-operative exposure does need a date to be modelled over time.

**Missing exposure status.** 891 patients (5.2%) have no pre-operative flag, and 731 (4.2%) are unknown on both fields. Unknown status is more common in development-era patients (14.1%) than in validation-era patients (12.2%), and 8 of 248 centres with TNM-complete patients have half or more unknown. Missing status is therefore clustered, not random (H07, H08).

**Eligibility.** The cohort is the 17,262 TNM-complete patients who had hepatectomy. Any treatment comparison will exclude patients with unknown treatment status. Treatment ND should not be coded as "no treatment" in the primary analysis, because ND is an explicit "not determined" entry.

**Immortal-time bias.** Any analysis in which post-operative chemotherapy is recorded as "received" must start follow-up at the point of treatment or use a landmark, because a patient must survive long enough to start it. A pre-operative exposure that is fixed before time zero (the operation) is not subject to this bias. This is the main reason to prefer the pre-operative definition as the primary comparison. A post-operative comparison requires a landmark or a time-varying Cox model with start dates, and 42% of those start dates are missing.

## 4. Which comparison is clinically meaningful?

Three candidate comparisons are assessed:

| Option | Identifiable? | Immortal time | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Surgery alone vs surgery plus perioperative chemotherapy | **Not as stated**: "surgery alone" cannot be distinguished from "no recorded liver chemotherapy", which includes unknown post-treatment. Post-operative exposure is not time-fixed. | Yes, for post-operative exposure | Not recommended as the primary question |
| B. **Pre-operative liver chemotherapy vs none (time zero = hepatectomy)** | Yes: flag, dated before surgery in 98.6% of dated cases | No | **Recommended primary** |
| C. Any liver chemotherapy before or after surgery vs none, with landmark | Partly: post-operative dates missing for 42% | Handled by landmark | Secondary, with the timing caveat |

Option B answers a clinically live question: whether a pre-operative strategy changes outcome relative to proceeding directly to surgery. Its comparator is "no pre-operative chemotherapy", which includes patients given chemotherapy only after surgery. That should be stated, not called "surgery alone".

A more specific strategy (for example, oxaliplatin-based versus irinotecan-based pre-operative regimens) is feasible only in part. Regimen components are recorded for 35–39% of patients, and the regimen fields are mostly missing for patients without pre-operative chemotherapy. Regimen comparisons are therefore exploratory and underpowered for the full cohort.

## 5. Confounding by indication and available pretreatment covariates

Chemotherapy is strongly linked to disease extent (H05). Among patients with known status, those treated before surgery differ from untreated patients:

| Covariate | Treated (any liver chemo) | Untreated | Standardised mean difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synchronous presentation | 64.6% | 42.4% | 0.46 |
| M1 | 64.4% | 41.5% | 0.47 |
| Metastasis count, median | 2 | 1 | 0.35 |
| Age, median | 62.6 | 66.3 | −0.33 |
| N2 | 29.1% | 23.8% | 0.12 |
| Pre-operative CEA, median | 6.5 | 9.0 | −0.03 |
| Largest metastasis, median mm | 30 | 30 | 0.07 |

Confounding by indication is therefore severe. Without adjustment, the untreated group is older, less synchronous and less extensively metastatic, so a crude comparison would be biased against chemotherapy.

**Available for adjustment (baseline, before treatment):** age, sex, T, N, M, synchrony, interval from primary diagnosis, metastasis count and size (with missing indicators), pre-operative CEA (65.8% recorded), era, and centre (as clusters).

**Not available (or not usable):** ECOG status, ASA, liver function, comorbidity (recorded for 4.9% of records, H10), KRAS (2.3%), BRAF (1.6%), MSI (0.8%), and extent of liver disease beyond metastasis count and size.

**Post-treatment variables that must not be used as adjusters:** resection margin (recorded for 60% of records; post-operative), lymph-node count from the primary, response (BESTREP), progression, and toxicity fields.

**Timing of metastasis count and size.** This is a critical open question. If the count or size was recorded after pre-operative chemotherapy (at restaging), it is a consequence of treatment, and adjusting for it would bias the treatment estimate. The same applies to the locked risk groups, which use these two variables. This must be confirmed with the registry data dictionary before any analysis that uses the groups as modifiers.

## 6. Are the risk groups suitable for treatment-effect modification?

**Strengths.** The groups are built from pre-operative variables (TNM, age, synchrony, interval, count and size). They are the locked, validated stratifier. Treatment is strongly concentrated in the Adverse group: 3,188 of 6,134 Adverse-group patients received pre-operative chemotherapy, against 1,969 of 5,483 Favourable-group patients (H06). Treatment and risk are therefore closely linked.

**Concern 1: post-treatment measurement.** The groups include metastasis count and size, which may have been recorded after pre-operative chemotherapy. If so, the Adverse group partly consists of patients whose lesions were reduced by treatment, and the groups would be measured after assignment. This is a collider and mediator problem.

**Concern 2: confounding within groups.** Confounding by indication persists within each group, because treated and untreated patients differ within group too. Stratifying on the groups does not remove this.

**Concern 3: power.** Crude death counts by group are: Favourable 1,048 deaths treated and 365 untreated; Intermediate 1,351 and 507; Adverse 1,873 and 525 (H09). Adequate events exist in each group, but weighted estimates will have wide intervals in the smaller untreated arms.

**Recommendation.** Use the locked groups **descriptively** and as an interaction term **only after** the timing of count and size is confirmed. If they were recorded before any treatment, the groups can be used as pre-specified effect modifiers. If not, interaction analysis should use a baseline-only stratifier (TNM, age, synchrony, interval), which would require a new, separately pre-specified model. That is new modelling and needs your approval.

## 7. Proposed statistical analysis plan

**Design.** Observational, retrospective, target-trial emulation: eligible patients at hepatectomy, treatment assignment at time zero (the operation), follow-up for overall survival. Reporting follows the TARGET statement (21 items, 2025; [target-guideline.org](https://target-guideline.org), [EQUATOR](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/22462/)) and STROBE.

**Population.** The 17,262-patient TNM-complete cohort. Exclude patients with unknown pre-operative status (891 ND or blank) from the primary analysis, and report the number excluded and their characteristics.

**Exposure (primary).** Pre-operative systemic chemotherapy for liver metastases (LE1PREOPMCTYN_F1 = Yes) versus no pre-operative chemotherapy (= No). Start date is used only to confirm it preceded surgery.

**Outcome.** Overall survival from hepatectomy, 15-year cap, as in the locked analysis. Secondary: recurrence-free survival using the same definition as the v8 notebook.

**Propensity model.** Logistic regression of pre-operative chemotherapy on baseline covariates: age (spline), sex, T, N, M, synchrony, interval (with missing indicator), metastasis count and size (with missing indicators, **only if their timing is confirmed to be pre-treatment**), pre-operative CEA (with missing indicator), era, and centre as a random or fixed effect if events allow. Pre-specified; no outcome-based selection.

**Estimation.** Stabilised inverse-probability-of-treatment weights, trimmed at the 1st and 99th percentiles, with the effective sample size reported. Overlap weights as a sensitivity analysis. Weighted Cox model for overall survival with robust (sandwich) variance; weighted Kaplan–Meier 5-year survival; restricted mean survival time at 5 years as a non-proportional-hazards summary.

**Balance diagnostics.** Standardised mean differences before and after weighting (target |SMD| < 0.10), variance ratios, a love plot, and effective sample size per arm. Balance is reported overall and within each prognostic group.

**Proportional hazards.** Scaled Schoenfeld residuals on the weighted model. If violated, report time-varying effects and RMST.

**Overall and by risk group.** Weighted HR (95% CI) overall and within Favourable, Intermediate and Adverse groups, with group sizes and event counts in each arm. Each group-specific estimate is reported with its own interval and is not interpreted in isolation.

**Formal interaction.** Weighted Cox model with treatment × risk-group terms (two product terms). Global likelihood-ratio test for heterogeneity of the treatment effect across the three groups. Group-specific p-values are not used to claim heterogeneity.

**Sensitivity analyses (pre-specified).**
1. Treat ND as "no treatment" (an upper bound on misclassification).
2. Exclude patients with post-operative chemotherapy from the comparator.
3. Propensity matching (1:1, caliper 0.2 SD of the logit) instead of weighting.
4. Overlap weights.
5. Propensity model without metastasis count and size (tests dependence on possible post-treatment measurement).
6. Post-operative chemotherapy as a time-varying exposure with a 3-month landmark (requires start dates; restricted to patients with dates or analysed with the missing-date indicator).
7. E-value for the observed HR, to quantify the unmeasured confounding needed to explain the result.
8. Negative-control outcome or exposure, if a suitable one is identified (for example, primary-tumour chemotherapy's association with a non-cancer outcome; none is present in the registry at present).

**Reporting.** Flow diagram of treated and untreated patients; covariate table before and after weighting; HRs with 95% CIs; event counts; group sizes; sensitivity results; limitations (below). No causal language; observational associations are reported as associations.

## 8. Limitations to state in the manuscript

1. Confounding by indication cannot be fully removed: ECOG, liver function, comorbidity and KRAS are absent or sparse.
2. Treatment assignment is observational; results cannot show benefit or lack of benefit.
3. Start dates are missing for about 42% of exposed patients, and unknown status is clustered by centre.
4. The meaning of "post-operative" differs between liver-metastasis and primary-tumour fields.
5. Regimen detail is partial; intensity, dose and response are not comparable across centres.
6. Metastasis count and size may be post-treatment; this must be verified.
7. Selection: the analytic cohort excludes 33.8% of hepatectomy patients (incomplete staging), and the excluded patients differ systematically (v8 Supplementary Table S5).
8. The registry is observational and single-registry; no external validation.

## 9. Decisions needed before any treatment analysis

1. **Primary question.** Approve pre-operative liver chemotherapy versus none (Option B) as primary, with surgery-first wording rather than "surgery alone".
2. **Data dictionary.** Confirm (a) whether metastasis count and size are recorded at diagnosis or at restaging, and (b) the exact meaning of POSTCOL and PRECOL fields.
3. **Interaction stratifier.** Either accept the locked groups (only if count and size pre-date treatment), or approve a new baseline-only stratifier, which is new modelling and must be pre-specified.
4. **Cohort for treatment.** Confirm the 17,262 cohort (unknown treatment excluded), or approve a multiple-imputation sensitivity for treatment status (not recommended as primary).
5. **Manuscript structure.** Whether the treatment comparison is a second paper or a second results section, given that the prognostic paper is now complete.

## 10. What can and cannot be concluded from this design

**Can be concluded, if the design is accepted:** whether pre-operative liver chemotherapy is associated with overall survival in a cohort adjusted for recorded baseline factors, with stated balance, sensitivity and effect-modification results.

**Cannot be concluded:** that chemotherapy benefits or harms patients, that the risk groups predict benefit from chemotherapy, or anything about surgery-alone versus perioperative strategies as usually defined.
